My doctoral dissertation (2019–2025) focuses on macroeconomics and firms’ access to finance, particularly the relationship between government borrowing and corporate financing decisions. Beginning in 2018, I collected annual reports and financial statements for 1,802 publicly listed and privately held firms across 29 African countries. Using web scraping techniques, followed by manual verification, I constructed a comprehensive firm-year panel dataset. By integrating these firm-level observations with macroeconomic data, I examined how domestic and external public debt affect corporate borrowing, capital structure, and financial conditions across African countries. The dataset and accompanying code are publicly available on my personal website (www.theogenehabimana.com). My current research includes cross-country studies of government debt and corporate capital structure, as well as a collaborative research project with researchers at Harvard University examining sovereign debt in Sub-Saharan Africa, supported by the Center for the Study of African Societies and Economies (CSASE) at Harvard University. My ongoing research also includes a project funded by the Foundation for Economic Education (LSR 2025, Application No. 32-16490), which investigates the role of artificial intelligence (AI) and cybersecurity risks in corporate financing decisions using machine learning and computational text analysis. As part of this project, I have developed firm-level measures of AI adoption and cybersecurity risk by systematically extracting and analyzing information from corporate annual reports of companies across 91 countries. These measures provide a foundation for examining how emerging technologies and cybersecurity-related risks influence firms’ financing decisions and capital structures in both developed and emerging markets

Research Interests

Sovereign debt and public finance; international macro-finance; financial development; corporate financing; Fin Tech;applied econometrics.

Current Research Projects

  • Sovereign Debt in Sub-Saharan Africa Project (ongoing). Research collaborator on a project sponsored by CSASE at Harvard University. The project examines sovereign debt accumulation, fiscal sustainability, public debt management, and the economic consequences of government borrowing in Sub-Saharan African countries. My interests within this collaboration include sovereign borrowing, private-sector credit, and corporate financing.
  • Rethinking Capital Structure Theories in the Age of Rising Public Debt: Crowding-Out versus Crowding-In (ongoing). Empirical analysis of government debt and corporate leverage using a global panel covering approximately 100 countries and more than 55,000 firms over 1990–2024, with attention to institutional quality and financial development.
