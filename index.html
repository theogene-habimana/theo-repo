Research Profile

My research examines how government borrowing affects firms’ access to finance. I use firm-level and macroeconomic data to study domestic and external public debt, corporate borrowing, and financial conditions across countries. My current work includes cross-country research on government debt and capital structure, as well as a research collaboration on sovereign debt in Sub-Saharan Africa through a project sponsored by the Center for the Study of African Societies and Economies (CSASE) at Harvard University.

Research Interests

Sovereign debt and public finance; international macro-finance; financial development; corporate financing; applied econometrics.

Current Research Projects

  • Sovereign Debt in Sub-Saharan Africa Project (ongoing). Research collaborator on a project sponsored by CSASE at Harvard University. The project examines sovereign debt accumulation, fiscal sustainability, public debt management, and the economic consequences of government borrowing in Sub-Saharan African countries. My interests within this collaboration include sovereign borrowing, private-sector credit, and corporate financing.
  • Rethinking Capital Structure Theories in the Age of Rising Public Debt: Crowding-Out versus Crowding-In (ongoing). Empirical analysis of government debt and corporate leverage using a global panel covering approximately 100 countries and more than 55,000 firms over 1990–2024, with attention to institutional quality and financial development.

Peer-Reviewed Publication

  • Colak, G., Habimana, T., & Korkeamäki, T. (2025). The effects of government debt on corporate borrowing in developing economies: Evidence from Africa. Journal of International Business Studies, 56, 874–900. DOI. The study covers 2,803 firms in 29 African countries.
